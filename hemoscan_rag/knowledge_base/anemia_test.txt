Anemia Diagnostic Tests and Lab InterpretationTo accurately diagnose and monitor anemia, healthcare providers use a series of blood tests to determine the count, size, shape, and health of red blood cells (RBCs), as well as the status of nutrient stores.1. Complete Blood Count (CBC)The CBC is the primary screening tool. It measures several components of the blood:Hemoglobin (Hgb): The oxygen-carrying protein. Low levels are the definitive indicator of anemia.Hematocrit (Hct): The percentage of total blood volume made up of RBCs.Red Blood Cell Count: The actual number of RBCs in a microliter of blood.Mean Corpuscular Volume (MCV): Measures the average size of the RBCs.Low MCV (Microcytic): Often iron deficiency or thalassemia.High MCV (Macrocytic): Often Vitamin B12 or Folate deficiency.Mean Corpuscular Hemoglobin (MCH): The average amount of hemoglobin per RBC.Red Cell Distribution Width (RDW): Measures the variation in RBC size. A high RDW means the cells vary greatly in size, which is an early sign of nutritional deficiency.2. Iron Status PanelIf the CBC suggests iron deficiency, these follow-up tests are required:Serum Ferritin: Measures the body’s iron stores. This is the most sensitive test for iron deficiency.Serum Iron: Measures the amount of iron currently in the liquid part of the blood (plasma).Total Iron-Binding Capacity (TIBC): Measures how well the protein "transferrin" can carry iron. In iron deficiency, TIBC is usually high (the body is "hungry" for iron).Transferrin Saturation: The percentage of transferrin that is actually saturated with iron.3. Vitamin Deficiency TestsUsed when cells are larger than normal (Macrocytic):Vitamin B12 Level: Checks for cobalamin deficiency.Serum Folate: Checks for Vitamin B9 levels.Methylmalonic Acid (MMA): A more sensitive test for B12 deficiency; levels rise if B12 is low.4. Advanced Diagnostic TestsFor complex or chronic cases where the cause is not a simple deficiency:Reticulocyte Count: Measures the number of young, newly formed RBCs. This tells the doctor if the bone marrow is responding correctly to the anemia.Peripheral Blood Smear: A technician looks at the blood under a microscope to check for abnormal shapes (e.g., sickle cells or "target cells" in thalassemia).Hemoglobin Electrophoresis: Identifies the type of hemoglobin in the blood; used to diagnose Sickle Cell Anemia and Thalassemia.Fecal Occult Blood Test (FOBT): Checks for hidden blood in the stool to see if the anemia is caused by slow GI bleeding (ulcers or polyps).5. Bone Marrow ExaminationIn rare or severe cases (like suspected Aplastic Anemia or Leukemia), a doctor may perform:Bone Marrow Aspiration: Removing a small amount of liquid bone marrow.Bone Marrow Biopsy: Removing a small core of bone marrow tissue to see how cells are being produced.Lab Reference Table (Summary)TestTypical Result in Iron DeficiencyTypical Result in B12/Folate DeficiencyHemoglobinLowLowMCV (Size)Low (Small cells)High (Large cells)FerritinLowNormal/HighRDWHighHighReticulocytesLow (Inadequate production)Low (Inadequate production)
